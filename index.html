I develop computational methods to make sense of clinical and biological data. My work combines machine learning, statistical modelling and explainable AI, with a focus on how reliable the results are and what they mean in practice.

At Imperial, my main research combines longitudinal multi-omics and clinical data to study pulmonary hypertension. I also contribute to My Heart Counts, studying how phone and watch data can support clinically useful measures of cardiovascular health. I work closely with clinicians to shape the questions, design the analysis and interpret the findings.

Research

Connected questions across data and clinical settings
01 / Imperial · Pulmonary hypertension

Longitudinal multi-omics and clinical data

My main research at Imperial integrates repeated proteomic and DNA methylation measurements with longitudinal clinical data in pulmonary hypertension. I investigate disease mechanisms and progression, with questions spanning early detection of clinical worsening, prediction and analysis of treatment response, and biological ageing. The central focus is on understanding how molecular and clinical changes unfold together over time.

02 / Imperial · My Heart Counts

Clinical insights from phones and watches

I recently joined My Heart Counts, contributing to research using phone and smartwatch data. My work examines how wear patterns affect the signals we observe and explores clinically useful measures that can be derived from these data, including estimating and forecasting six-minute walk distance (6MWD).

03 / Biomarker discovery

Finding stable biological signals

Working with the University of Padova, I developed similarity-based feature selection and SHAP-based binarisation methods for microbiome data. In inflammatory bowel disease datasets, these approaches improved stability or interpretability while maintaining or improving predictive performance.

04 / Wearable measurements

Evaluating smartwatch blood pressure monitoring

In collaboration with clinical teams and the Korean Society of Hypertension, I led computational analyses of smartwatch blood pressure data. This research examines calibration effects, measurement stability and individual differences, and develops post-processing approaches to improve the usefulness of watch-based measurements.

05 / Clinical decision support

Supporting decisions in influenza-like illness

Working with paediatric clinicians at Korea University Hospital, I developed machine learning models for influenza-like illness using clinical records from more than 2,500 patients. I designed a real-time decision-support pipeline connecting patient questionnaires with model predictions for clinical consultations, and now advise on analyses of newly collected data.

06 / Explainable AI

Assessing the validity of model explanations

I study how sample size, feature coverage and model performance affect the validity and stability of feature importance in biomedical machine learning. In a related project, CLE-SH, I directed the study design and analytical approach and supervised implementation of a Python toolkit for statistical interpretation of SHAP values.
